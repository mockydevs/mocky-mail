# Mocky Mail 0.10.0

A security release: see who really sent a message, and keep private mail
private.

One line for the update notice (`latest.json`):

> Signed and encrypted email with S/MIME and OpenPGP.

## Signed mail

- Messages signed with S/MIME or OpenPGP show a bar above them: signed by
  a verified sender, signed but the signer isn't verified, changed after it
  was signed, or can't be checked. Details show who signed it, who vouched
  for them and when. Mocky Mail tells you when the signer isn't the sender
  shown.
- S/MIME certificates are checked against the authorities your computer
  trusts for email, and old, weak signatures are refused.
- For OpenPGP, trust a sender's key once you've compared its fingerprint
  with them.
- Signed and encrypted messages carry a seal or a lock in the message list.

## Encrypted mail

- Encrypted S/MIME and OpenPGP mail opens on this device when you have the
  key. If you don't, Mocky Mail says why.
- Sign and encrypt what you send from compose's options. Mocky Mail checks
  that it has a key for every recipient (and looks OpenPGP keys up from
  their mail provider), and asks what to do about anyone it can't encrypt
  to. Your copy in Sent stays readable to you. The subject isn't encrypted.
- Choose to sign every message, or encrypt whenever every recipient's key
  is known, in Settings › Security.

## Keys and certificates

- Settings › Security lists your keys and certificates and the ones
  collected from people who write to you. Import a certificate (.p12, .pfx,
  .cer) or an OpenPGP key (.asc), export your public one to share, and
  choose which one each account uses.
- Your private keys are stored encrypted, unlocked through your system's
  password store.
- Certificates in the Windows certificate store and keys in GnuPG are
  listed and used to check signatures; to sign or decrypt with them, export
  them and import them into Mocky Mail.

## Good to know

- Search doesn't look inside encrypted messages.
- Whether a certificate has been revoked isn't checked yet.
